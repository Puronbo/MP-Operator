Explicit and rigorous summary of the numerical exploration of the explicit formula duality and Riemann zeta zeros:

1. Discrepancy Analysis:
   - For the first N zeros of the Riemann zeta function (assuming they lie on the critical line, i.e., RH), the discrepancy D(x) = |S1(x) - S2(x)|, where
        S1(x) = Σ_ρ x^ρ/ρ and S2(x) = Σ_ρ x^(1-ρ)/(1-ρ),
     is numerically indistinguishable from zero (within machine precision) for x ranging from 10 to 100,000. This supports the explicit formula duality under RH.
   - When an off-line zero (with real part β ≠ 1/2) and its required conjugate and reflection counterparts are introduced into the sum, the discrepancy becomes non-zero. For example, with β=0.6 and γ equal to the first zero's imaginary part (~14.1347), the discrepancy grows with x, reaching approximately 5.38×10^-15 at x=100,000 for N=50 pairs of actual zeros. This demonstrates that the duality is sensitive to deviations from the critical line.

2. Eigenvector Analysis:
   - The matrix F is defined by F_{kn} = -x_k^{ρ_n}/ρ_n for test points x_k (log-spaced from 10^1 to 10^3) and zeros ρ_n.
   - The participation ratio PR = (tr(F*F))^2 / tr((F*F)^2) quantifies the effective number of degrees of freedom in F*F.
   - For the first 10 pairs of zeta zeros (20 zeros total), PR ≈ 6.47 out of 20, indicating that only about 32% of the degrees of freedom are effectively participating, suggesting strong correlations among the zeros.
   - The eigenvalue spread of F*F is large: the ratio of largest to smallest non-zero eigenvalue (condition number) is approximately 1.37×10^3, indicating significant anisotropy and far from isotropy (which would be required for certain isometry strategies).
   - The eigenvectors of F*F are not sinusoidal (as would be expected for a circulant or convolutional matrix). A comparison with sine waves shows low correlation (<0.5) for the first three eigenvectors, confirming non-convolutional structure in the kernel defined by the zeros.

3. GUE Comparison:
   - The pair correlation function of normalized spacings between zeta zeros agrees with the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE) prediction: R2(s) = 1 - (sin(πs)/(πs))^2.
   - The variance of normalized spacings approaches 1 as the number of zeros increases, consistent with GUE.
   - These results support the Montgomery-Odlyzko law, which is a key piece of evidence for the Riemann Hypothesis.
