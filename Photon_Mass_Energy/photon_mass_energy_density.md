# Photon Mass and Energy Density for Luminescence Sources in Skin

## Assumptions
- Source power \(P\) ranges from 1 nW to 1 mW.
- Wavelengths \(\lambda\) = 400, 500, 600, 700, 800 nm.
- Photons travel through a skin thickness \(L = 1\) mm.
- Beam cross‑section taken as \(A = 1\) mm² → illuminated volume \(V = A \cdot L = 1\) mm³ = \(1\times10^{-9}\) m³.
- Residence time in the volume \(\tau = L / c\) (time to traverse the thickness at speed of light).
- Photon energy \(E_\gamma = hc/\lambda\).
- Photon rate (flux) \(\Phi = P / E_\gamma\) (photons s⁻¹).
- Energy density \(u = \Phi \, E_\gamma \, \tau / V\) (J m⁻³).
- Photon mass density \(\rho_\gamma = u / c^2\) (kg m⁻³).

## Interpretation
- The photon energy density \(u\) scales linearly with power and is independent of wavelength (because the product \(\Phi E_\gamma = P\) cancels the wavelength dependence). Indeed, \(u = P \tau / V\) depends only on power, thickness, and cross‑section.
- The equivalent mass density \(\rho_\gamma = u/c^2\) is extremely small: even at 1 mW it is only \(\sim 3.7\times10^{-23}\) kg m⁻³ (\(3.7\times10^{-26}\) g cm⁻³), many orders of magnitude below the density of water (~1 g cm⁻³). Thus the photon field’s inertia is negligible for mechanical effects on skin.
- However, the energy density (though small) can still deposit dose when absorbed; the next steps compute the deposited dose via Monte‑Carlo simulation.
