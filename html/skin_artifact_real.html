Skin Luminescence & Q-Luminance Artifact

This artifact visualizes the optical properties of skin layers (epidermis, dermis, subcutis) and the resulting luminescence transmission requirements for detectable signals. It also shows the Q-luminescence parameter (expected detectable count rate) as a function of source power and wavelength.

Key assumptions:

  • Layer thicknesses: epidermis 0.1 mm, dermis 2.0 mm, subcutis 5.0 mm (total 7.1 mm). For thinner total thicknesses, layers are truncated in order.
  • Optical properties: wavelength-dependent absorption and scattering coefficients based on literature (Jacques et al.).
  • Detection efficiency: quantum efficiency 0.80, collection efficiency 0.10 → overall η = 0.08.
  • Detection threshold: 1 detectable photon per second.

1. Skin Transmittance vs Wavelength and Thickness

Shows how transmittance varies with wavelength for three total skin thicknesses: 0.1 mm (epidermis only), 1.0 mm (epidermis + dermis), and 3.0 mm (epidermis + dermis + part of subcutis).

2. Required Source Power for 1 Detectable Count per Second

Shows the optical power needed to achieve one detectable count per second (after detector efficiency) as a function of wavelength for the three thicknesses. Lower power means easier detection.

3. Q_L Contour (Detectable Count Rate)

Contour plot of log10(Q_L) where Q_L is the expected detectable count rate (counts per second). Q_L = (P / E) * T * η. The region Q_L ≥ 1 (log10(Q_L) ≥ 0) corresponds to detectable signals. For typical source powers (≥ 1 nW), Q_L is many orders of magnitude above 1 (inflicted sector).
